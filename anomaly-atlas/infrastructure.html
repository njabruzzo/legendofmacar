Historical infrastructure comparison / November 2022–November 2026

Are major system failures
becoming more common?

Not consistently. Industry surveys indicate fewer outages per site, but the publicly reported incidents that do occur can be more damaging, and more services rely on shared external infrastructure.

Three findings with different denominators; they must not be combined into one invented “global anomaly score.”

The distinction: A single provider can fail once and disrupt many organizations. A widely reported cluster may be real in consequences without representing an unusual number of independent underlying failures. The casebook below is selected examples only, not a total-event count.

What the evidence supports

It supports investigating systemic concentration and the consequences of shared dependencies. It also supports examining whether some incident classes, such as cyberattack-related outages, have become more damaging.

It does not demonstrate that all infrastructure failures are accelerating, that this selection is statistically representative, or that the incidents share an actor or cause.

How the next baseline should work

  • Choose stable reporting systems with complete historical coverage for each sector.
  • Keep incident definitions and thresholds fixed across years.
  • Count upstream causes once, rather than counting each downstream report as another event.
  • Adjust for exposure, seasonality, data coverage, and reporting changes.
  • Use same-length windows and a held-out baseline to test clustering.
